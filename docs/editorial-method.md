# Editorial Method

The briefing is a daily decision-support product, not a news digest. Each issue must identify what changed, explain why the evidence matters, and define the next observable trigger without repeating facts already covered.

## Coverage contract

Select exactly 10 high-signal stories across:

- AI and technology;
- financial markets and investing;
- healthcare and medtech.

Maintain a useful mix across the three domains. No company may occupy more than two stories, and no broad theme should consume more than roughly 20–30% of the issue.

Prioritize developments relevant to AI products, semiconductors, market positioning, electrophysiology, medical devices, and practical capital allocation. Favor evidence over commentary.

## Source hierarchy

Start with primary and accountable sources:

1. Company filings, earnings materials, and investor-relations releases.
2. FDA, regulator, and government notices.
3. Policy documents, clinical publications, and trial records.
4. Market prices, fund-flow data, and other direct measurements.
5. Credible reporting that adds verified facts or necessary context.

Use commentary only when it contributes an original, decision-relevant interpretation. Reject generic trend pieces, recycled opinions, sensational headlines, and claims without operational or investment relevance.

## Seven-day review

Before research selection, read the prior seven briefings and load two rolling ledgers.

### Story ledger

Track:

- stable story ID;
- topic and company;
- first-covered and last-covered dates;
- facts already reported;
- prior interpretation;
- next trigger;
- active or inactive state.

### Glossary ledger

Track the term, the last date it received a full explanation, contexts already explained, and whether a materially new context justifies another explanation.

The ledgers are editorial memory. They prevent the model from treating a newly phrased headline as a new fact.

## Candidate selection

For every candidate, ask:

1. **Novelty:** Is the underlying fact new to the last seven issues?
2. **Consequence:** Could it change an operating decision, investment view, policy path, clinical interpretation, or competitive position?
3. **Evidence:** Is there a reliable source and enough detail to explain the claim accurately?
4. **Reader relevance:** Is it connected to the reader's priorities rather than merely popular?
5. **Next trigger:** Is there a concrete event, data release, filing, decision, or market level to watch?

Select at least seven genuinely new stories. Use no more than three continuing-story updates.

## Rules for returning stories

A story may return only because of a hard catalyst, meaningful new data, or a thesis-changing development. Theme repetition is acceptable; fact repetition is not.

Apply default cooldowns:

- ordinary company news: 3 days;
- broad industry commentary: 5–7 days;
- the same product introduction: 7 days.

Remove a continuing story after three days without a meaningful update. Compress prior background to one or two sentences.

For every returning story, state:

- What's new today / 今日新增
- What has not changed / 未改变的判断
- Why this changes the picture / 为什么值得再提
- Next trigger / 下一观察节点

If the new evidence does not change understanding or action, do not include the story.

## Story identity and status

Assign a stable category-prefixed ID such as `AI-01`, `MKT-02`, or `MED-03`. Reuse the same ID when a continuing narrative returns; do not create a new identity merely because the headline changed.

Assign exactly one bilingual status:

- `NEW / 全新`
- `UPDATE / 进展`
- `THESIS CHANGE / 判断变化`
- `WATCH / 观察中`

## Writing template

Write each story in this order:

1. **10-second takeaway / 10秒看懂** — the conclusion in plain language.
2. **Why Will should care / 为什么与你有关** — the practical connection.
3. **What happened / 发生了什么** — verified facts and relevant numbers.
4. **Why it matters / 为什么重要** — consequence, mechanism, and comparison.
5. **Signal vs. noise / 信号还是噪音** — what is durable and what remains uncertain.
6. **Next trigger / 下一观察节点** — a concrete event or observable threshold.
7. **Ask next / 可以继续追问** — one to three useful bilingual follow-up questions.

Write natural professional English first, then a complete and natural Chinese rendering. Do not translate word for word when professional phrasing differs between languages. Do not hide substantive analysis only in Chinese.

## Terminology policy

Add a key-term card only when a moderately technical term could block understanding or materially alter interpretation. Explain:

- what it is;
- what it means in this story;
- what it should not be confused with.

Check the glossary ledger before repeating a full explanation. A familiar term may be explained again only when the new context changes its practical meaning.

End the issue with five terms. Each entry needs an English term, Chinese name, plain-language definition, an authentic English usage example, and a natural Chinese translation of that exact example. Every example must demonstrate the term's actual meaning; template filler is not acceptable.

## Final pages

Finish with:

1. **Today's Watchlist / 今日关注** — five to eight concise and actionable items.
2. **Five Terms Today / 今日五个关键词** — specific usage examples, not placeholders.
3. **Sources & Method / 来源与方法** — source name, publication date, clickable link, and a note describing editorial selection and anti-repetition.

## Editorial QA

Before layout, verify:

- There are exactly 10 stories.
- At least 7 are new and no more than 3 are updates.
- No repeated fact is presented as new.
- No company exceeds two stories.
- No broad theme dominates the issue.
- Every story has a stable ID, status, next trigger, and follow-up question.
- Claims and dates match their cited sources.
- English is complete and leads every content stack.
- Term examples are semantically specific and not recycled templates.

After publication, update both ledgers with the final facts, interpretation, terms, and triggers actually used in the issue.
